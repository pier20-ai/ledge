NewReframed — no countdown calendar · agent · mini · notify

Copilot. The countdown is gone. A ticking clock to your next meeting is a deadline made continuously visible — it creates pressure and offers no agency, which is a working definition of anxiety. So the signature inverts: the number is your runway, the uninterrupted time you own right now. “2h 40m clear” is a fact you can act on; “T−11:52” is a bomb timer.

The ribbon inverts too — the free blocks are the figure, the meetings are the ground. And the agent does the two jobs no calendar app does: it briefs you before (who, what you last agreed, what you owe them) and it captures after, which is the most under-served moment in the whole category. The mini appears twice a meeting — once at two minutes to join, once when it ends — and never in between.

Ask the builder: “when a meeting has no agenda, draft one from the invite thread.”

NewReframed — rules, not files intake drop · capture · agent · spotlight

Drop. The honest question is what this beats about just talking to Claude — and the answer is that it isn't a faster way to ask, it's a way to stop asking. A chat is a conversation you re-open and re-explain every time. Drop is a standing order: you teach it once, in plain English, and it applies forever, silently, while you keep working. It also finishes the job — chat tells you what's in the PDF; Drop renames the file, files it, and closes the loop.

So the interface inverts: the files aren't the content, the rules are. Files pass through as events; the durable thing is the little machine you built. The panel is a workshop, not a folder — and the only time it interrupts you is when something arrives that no rule covers, which is exactly the moment to offer to make one.

Ask the builder: “when I drop a CSV, chart it and keep the chart next to the file.”

Rework · absorbs stocks + trader canvas candles · agent writes code · notify · sqlite

Desk. No broker, no orders, no paper P&L pretending to be trading — the old app's weakest part was the transaction fiction. What replaces it is better: the agent here doesn't just opine, it writes and runs the analysis. You ask a question in English; it fetches the history, computes, and the answer comes back as a chart in the app.

That's the novel claim — no terminal on earth lets you invent a new study by asking for it. A study you like becomes a permanent panel, so you grow your own Bloomberg one question at a time. Candles are cheap for us: a body is a rect, a wick is a line — the two ops we already ship. Slow and qualitative by design: daily candles, end-of-day notes, and unprompted context only when a move is genuinely outside its range.

Ask the builder: “keep this study on the panel, and re-run it every Friday close.”

Newcanvas drag (A1) + scroll-zoom (A8) · sprites · gradient op

Weather — and this one is a working prototype, not a picture. Drag the ruler to move time; scroll over it to switch hours ↔ days. The bottom bar is the clock's scrubber idiom again — rulers are becoming a Ledge signature — and the scene at the right re-derives continuously as you drag.

The thing that makes this feasible is a single decision: the scene is a pure function of (solar elevation, cloud cover, precipitation, temperature), and all four are continuous. So scrubbing doesn't cross-fade between canned illustrations — the sun genuinely slides down its arc, clouds thicken, rain ramps up, the sky re-tints. Only two things are discrete (the umbrella and the sunglasses) and those are the only things that fade. Layers composite per frame: sky gradient → sun/moon on its solar arc → drifting clouds → rain → character → ground.

Ask the builder: “peek me ten minutes before sunset on clear days.”
